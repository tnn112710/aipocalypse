# World map environment

Scene: `Assets/_Game/Scenes/Prototypes/WorldMap.unity` (a prototype scene: the mechanics are tested here). Layout: concept v13 (`docs/concepts/world-map-top-down-v13.png`, notes in `world-map.md`); the generator's `WorldMapLayout.cs` is traced from it. The v23 files in `docs/concepts/` (`world-map-top-down-v23.png`, `world-map-v23-prompt.md`) are a later concept the current WorldMap is not built from. Art target: the Raygeas packs (Komorebi Nature, HIGHLANDS, Grasslands, Lakefell Village) with BK Pure Nature as support; reference screenshots in `docs/concepts/refs/`.

Everything is generated by editor scripts in `AgentScripts/Maps/WorldMap/` (outside `Assets/`, run with `unity command run_script`), so the layout can be changed and rebuilt. How to work on it (and on any other map) is in the skill `tools-unity-map-generation`; MicroVerse use here is in that skill (`references/microverse-in-this-project.md`, library detail in the user-scope `unity-microverse`); Stylized Water details are in `tools-unity-stylized-water`. What only this map uses (TerrainData, heightmaps and masks, water meshes, backdrop, navmesh) lives in `Assets/_Game/Maps/WorldMap/`. Reusable first-party copies (water materials, terrain layers, vegetation, rocks, mountain materials) live in the shared kit `Assets/_Game/Environment/`, so another map can use them. Time-of-day data: `Assets/_Game/Data/Environment/TimeOfDay/`.

## World layout

One terrain, 1000 × 300 × 1000 m at the origin, heightmap 2049 (about 0.49 m), alphamap 2048, detail resolution 1024. North is +Z. The playable area is the `Playable` polygon in the generator (about X 155–964, Z 66–943); outside it is backdrop. `WorldMapLayout.Preview(1024, path)` renders the layout (heights, water, region outlines, trails) to a PNG without touching any asset.

Rivers and streams vary in width (about ±30%) and depth along their length (pools and runs: deeper where narrower). Their ends keep the designed width. Each `Line` builds its smoothed points lazily from its knots.

Player spawn: inside the gate on the gate→bridge line (`HomeSpawn` ≈ (272.0, 289.8)), facing the bridge (yaw ≈ 29).

**Home base.** `HomeBase`, `HomeYaw` and `FenceHalf` are the only inputs. `Yard`/`YardLocal`/`FenceDistance`, `GateSlot` (the front-fence slot the gate→bridge line crosses), `HomeGate` and `HomeSpawn` derive from them; the masks, fence, gate, house and props, the trails' first knots and the spawn follow. Moving the base needs a full rebuild and a re-check of `Anchors`, `MeadowHills` and `HomeBaseHeight`.

## How it is built (`WorldMapLayout.cs` + `Atmosphere.cs`)

Order of a full rebuild (entry points of `WorldMapLayout.cs` unless noted):

0. Kit `MicroVerseKit.Enable(true)`. Between rebuilds the scene keeps MicroVerse disabled (baked mode), so opening the scene, recompiling or saving never regenerates or rewrites `Terrain_0_0.asset` (tools-unity-map-generation → "Rebuild discipline"). Kit `Rebuild` refuses while it is disabled.
1. `Generate(2048, 1024, preview)`: analytic height field → `Terrain/Heightmaps/Base.png` (R16) plus one mask per Rowlan detail stamp. Polygons and water lines at the top of the file are the layout; edit them there.
   - Water is carved into the field: basins (lake, ponds) and lines (river, streams) with a shelved channel profile; banks never dip below the water surface.
   - Rowlan detail stamps (`Details` table: Highlands 09/07, Meadows 05/16/26, Swamp 12) are `Add` stamps at Y = 0. The shader clamps stamp heights at 0, so the base map is lowered by `median × amplitude × mask` to make them zero-mean.
2. `BuildHeights()`: the MicroVerse `Height` group: macro base (Override) → region detail → home pad.
3. Kit `MicroVerseKit.Rebuild(false)` to sync heights before anything samples the terrain.
4. `GenerateTextureMasks(1024)`, `BuildTerrainLayers()`, `BuildTextures()`: 8 first-party layer copies (Grasslands grass/blue/yellow/mud/mudstones/sand, wet mud, a Komorebi rock-detail layer) and 20 texture stamps: base grass, cool patches, forest dirt, warm meadow, cool meadow patches, highland scree, swamp grass/mud, water beds, shore pebbles, beach coves, channel beds and banks, steep rock, highland shelves, and trails (a frayed bare core, `TrailHalfWidth` about 0.9–1.7 m; a patchy trampled verge; darker wear down the middle). Trails are not carved into the heightmap. Bed/shore follow the real waterline, so islands stay grassy.
5. `Vegetation.GenerateMasks(1024)`, `Vegetation.MakeVariants()`, `Vegetation.Build()`: the MicroVerse `Vegetation` group (details below).
6. `BuildBoundary(1337)`: per-side foot rocks, the near range with its pines, peaks, haze and a backdrop ground ring (models, no terrain raise) plus invisible walls. `BoundaryAngles(3)` reports, per viewpoint and 10° bearing, whether the peaks rise above the layers in front.
7. `BuildWaterMaterials()`, `BuildFlatWater()`, `BuildRivers()`, `BuildUnderwater()`: Stylized Water 3 (below). `BuildLakeReflection()` is not run while water reflections are off.
8. `Landmarks.Build(7)` (home base, bridge, trail signs; `BuildBridge()` is the bridge alone), `Landmarks.BuildWaterfall(206, 433, 100, 41.6, 19.5)`, `Outcrops.Build(5)`, `Dressing.BuildWaterEdges(21)`, `Dressing.BuildBanks(23)`, `Dressing.BuildHomePond(41)`, `Finishing.Islands(9)`.
9. `Atmosphere.cs`: `ClonePostProcessing()`, `Apply()`, `Particles()`; then `Vegetation.Wind()` (also adds `BK Foliage Globals`) **before** `Atmosphere.SharedWind()` (`Wind()` deletes the Wind root that `SharedWind()` fills), `Finishing.FillLight(0.45)` (skip it: the user deleted the fill light), `Finishing.PlayerSpawn()`. Don't run `Atmosphere.RenderQuality()`: it loads the URP asset from the old `Assets/Settings/` path, writes values the project has since changed and calls `AssetDatabase.SaveAssets()`. Then `AgentScripts/DayNight/DayNightSetup.Scene()`: `Particles()` rebuilds the atmosphere groups without their time-of-day reactors, and `Apply()` puts back the old static skybox ([DAYNIGHT.md](DAYNIGHT.md)).
10. `Vegetation.GenerateClearMasks(1024)` first, so the first tree pass already has this layout's gaps (it matters whenever the yard, trails or rocks move), kit `Rebuild(true)` (terrain trees exist from here), then `Dressing.BuildMeadow(31)` (keeps its stones and hero trees off terrain trees), `Dressing.BuildHomeTrees()`, `Vegetation.GenerateClearMasks(1024)` (after every rock or dressing pass), kit `Rebuild(true)`, draw distances (the saved scene uses detail 100 and tree 1000 with terrain shadows off; `Vegetation.DrawDistances(120, 240)` sets only the detail distance and BK render distance: pass the values you want and read them back), kit `Enable(false)`, `SaveScene("")`. Then check that every `Atmosphere/Leaf Fall` point still has a tree within 5 m (the points are hand-placed in `Particles()`).
11. `OpenScene()` reloads the saved scene. Do this before screenshots: the GPU Resident Drawer keeps drawing objects that scripts deleted or disabled until the scene reloads.
12. `Navigation.BuildWaterVolume(0.5, true)`, `Navigation.Bake(0.3)`, save (see "Navigation" below). Re-run `Bake` after any later step that adds, moves or removes colliders.

**Water-only rebuild.** The scene has hand edits made after the generator last ran in full (see "Generator drift" below), so a full rebuild would wipe them. After changing the pond or river, rebuild only what depends on the water, with `AgentScripts/Maps/WorldMap/RiverLevel.cs` around it:
1. Copy the terrain data to a temporary asset (the old heights) and `RiverLevel.Snapshot(file)` (which rock groups are shadowless).
2. Steps 0–3, `GenerateTextureMasks(1024)` + `BuildTextures()`, `Vegetation.GenerateMasks(1024)`, `BuildFlatWater()`, `BuildRivers()`, `BuildUnderwater()`, kit `Rebuild(false)`, `Dressing.BuildBanks(23)`, `Dressing.BuildHomePond(41)`, `Landmarks.BuildBridge()` (and `BuildWaterfall` if the fall or the pond level moved), `RiverLevel.Apply(file)`.
3. `Verify(dir)` (no new leaks or low banks), `RiverLevel.Drowned(19.5, 30, false)` (placed objects now under water: delete the ones that should not be there), `RiverLevel.Settle(oldTerrain, 60, 0.15, true)` (lowers placed objects by the ground's drop), `RiverLevel.Floating(40, 0.4)`.
4. `Vegetation.GenerateClearMasks(1024)`, kit `Rebuild(true)`, kit `Enable(false)`, `Navigation.BuildWaterVolume(0.5, true)`, `Navigation.Bake(0.3)`, save, `OpenScene()`, delete the temporary terrain copy. `RiverLevel.cs` keeps its own copy of the river knots and the pond centre: update it with the layout.

Gotcha: a basin's bank cone (`level + bankSlope × distance`, reaching 120 m) caps the ground around it. A big, low basin next to higher water cuts that water's banks (the 2.5× home pond at the first slope 0.3 dropped the West Pond rim 6.5 m and a West Stream bank 5 m); `Verify` reports it as leak cells or negative bank heights.

The scripted object groups (rocks, dressing, landmarks) sample terrain heights when placed: rebuild them after reshaping the terrain. MicroVerse stamps regenerate by themselves. `Verify(outDir)` checks basin depths, shore rims, channel depths, region heights and slopes, and the home pad. `Delta()` shows how much the detail stamps move each region.

## Navigation

Enemies walk one A* `RecastGraph` over the whole terrain, held by the scene's `Navigation` prefab instance (root object) and loaded at startup from `Maps/WorldMap/Navigation/WorldMapNavmesh.bytes`, so play never scans. Both steps are in the generator's `Navigation` class:

- `BuildWaterVolume(cell, wadeStreams)` writes `Maps/WorldMap/Navigation/NavmeshWater.asset` and `Environment/Water/Navmesh/Navmesh Water`: columns from 30 m under the water surface up to it, wherever the ground lies under water (`WaterSurfaceAt` + the real terrain height, the dressing masks' wet test), merged into runs along X. A `RecastNavmeshModifier` (Unwalkable, solid, always included) feeds it to the scan; its renderer is disabled (it has no material). It is needed because the underwater areas are trigger boxes, which the scan skips, so without it agents walk the lake and river beds. With `wadeStreams` (used) the streams, 1–1.3 m deep and crossed on foot like the player, stay walkable: blocking them cut 13% of the map (the land between the West, Forest and Margin streams and the river) off from the rest. Lakes, ponds, the river and the outlet are blocked; 0.17 km² at 0.5 m cells, ~15 s.
- `Bake(cellSize)` replaces the instance's graphs with one recast graph (0.3 m cells, 128-voxel tiles of 38 m, agent radius 0.5 m to match the enemies' `FollowerEntity`, height 2 m, climb 0.4 m, slope 45°, `Default` layer colliders + terrain + terrain trees), scans (~0.5 s), writes the cache and turns on "Cache startup". Result 2026-10-24: ~43k triangles, 1.3 MB; 90% of the walkable area is one connected region with home in it; the rest is lake islands, the backdrop beyond the playable edge and small tops of props.

The cache is a snapshot: re-run `Bake` after anything that adds, moves or removes colliders (dressing, landmarks, boundary, trees) and `BuildWaterVolume` first after the water or terrain changes. The A* inspector's Scan / Generate cache also work (the water mesh is a scene object), but `Bake` also resets the graph settings. Tiles keep later carving cheap (`NavmeshCut` for barricades and built walls).

## Boundary

Like the concept, the valley is enclosed by grey rocky mountains right at its edge. No terrain is raised for them: meshes stand in for raised ground. Layers rise one behind the other, so the far snow peaks still show over the near range. Anchors and assets come from a research pass over every rock and mountain pack in `_StoreAssets` (scratchpad `research6/brief.md`).

- `NearRange` rows are (id, x, z, footprint radius, crest height, yaw, jitter, prefab). The build scales them up after a first pass read too low behind the trees: radius ×1.15, crest ×1.3 (row A and fill knobs) or ×1.15 (back row); the table above gives the built heights. Each massif is scaled from its prefab's measured LOD0 mesh (`MassifInfo`): XZ to the radius (±15%), Y to the crest height. It sits below the lowest ground around it, is mirrored on every second use of the same prefab, and is pushed outward 3 m at a time (then shrunk) until no visible vertex comes within 6 m of the edge (back row 40 m), 37 m of the outlet river or 25 m of the waterfall pool (`MassifClear`). Colliders are removed (the ANGRY MESH mountains carry 14k-triangle mesh colliders) and shadows are off.
- Komorebi pines in five clumps per near massif (row A and fill knobs), raycast onto its valley-facing slopes (normal.y ≥ 0.7, lower 60% of the crest height); about 220 in all. `GenerateClearMasks` clears terrain trees under the massifs.
- Inside the edge, no forest or `Foothills` trees grow in the meadow or within 10–22 m of it, so the meadow runs to the foot rocks and the near range shows from it.
- Most south foot rocks are grass-topped first-party HIGHLANDS variants (`Rocks/HL_Rock_1/2_Grass`): the pack's own coverage grass renders teal, so the copy uses the Grasslands lawn texture. The Komorebi stones that face the home (W-A33, W-A35 and the west foot rocks within 100 m) use their `Rocks Grass` twin materials, so they read grass-topped.
- `Rock Feet`: young pines, small broadleafs and bushes where the massifs facing the meadow meet the ground on its valley side (raycast to each massif's foot, own random sequence so the range keeps its layout). Wild grass also runs 1–30 m past the playable edge, so the lawn doesn't stop at the invisible wall.
- ANGRY MESH materials are first-party copies (`Mountains/AM_*.mat`): the vendor rocks are beige and the mountains mauve, so the rocks are a warm neutral grey and the mountains desaturated. The rock copies keep the pack's grass top layer as caps on faces under ~50° (mesh-UV mapped, tiling 20 for the ×14–25 domes); it multiplies by the pack's shader globals, which `ASP Top Layer Globals` (`_Core/EnvironmentSystem/ASPTopLayerGlobals`) under `Environment/Boundary` sets. The mountain copies keep it off.
- Corners mix the two neighbouring foot-rock families: each cluster picks its side from the outward normal with ±25° of jitter.
- HIGHLANDS mountain tiles are whole ranges with a low side (M1 −Z, M2 +Z, M3 +X, M4 −Z). Yaw = direction to the map − low side, which turns the gentle slopes toward the player.
- Peaks sit 4 m below the lowest ground under their footprint and are pushed outward until no visible vertex is within 40 m of the playable area.
- The HIGHLANDS and Grasslands mountain shaders ignore scene fog; their own height haze (`_FogColor`) is teal. `Mountains/HL_/GL_*.mat` are copies retinted to the sky haze. The ANGRY MESH shader takes scene fog. Scene fog is 0.0011.
- `Haze`: Komorebi `Fog_1` banks toward the peaks (placed from each mountain's final, pushed-out position).
- `Outlet Gorge`: big Grasslands slabs on both banks where the outlet river leaves the map and one across its end, so the river seems to run on between rocks.
- `Backdrop Ground`: a ring mesh from the terrain border out to 2.5 km, just under the border heights (a low percentile, so the outlet's channel doesn't drag it down), so no gap between meshes shows the void.
- Invisible walls: 220 box colliders along the playable edge on `Ignore Raycast` (they stop the player, not shots). Keep-out zones keep rocks off the home base, viewpoint, waterfall lip and pond, bridge and streams.
- The player camera's far clip is 1500 m: keep peaks within about 1.4 km of the viewpoints.
- Earlier versions: tall Komorebi rock rows hid the peaks; then low foot rocks with distant ridges looked like a plain. The near range is the concept's enclosed valley.

## Water (Stylized Water 3)

- Flat bodies (lake, three ponds, swamp, the waterfall's source pool) are `WaterObject`s on meshes generated from the shore outlines: 2 m cells kept where at least one corner is under water, connected to the basin's interior, and, beyond the shore, not beside a lower stream (inside the shore the basin's water always holds, so an outflow never cuts the sheet in a grid staircase; the sheet fades out under the outflow's ribbon instead) (a flood fill, so low ground beyond a rim never gets a sheet of water). Transform Y is the water level. Meshes: `Maps/WorldMap/Water/*.asset`.
- Basins have a 0.35 m lip to 6 m out that then falls away at about 19°, so a pond on a slope sits in a bowl instead of perching on a ridge.
- Rivers and streams are `RiverModeler` ribbons (`Environment/Water/Rivers`: River, Outlet, Streams) on the same knots that carved the beds, plus 0.8 m so the edges tuck under the banks. West and Margin streams form one ribbon; the forest stream and the swamp outlet fade in and out over it. Don't link knots between ribbons: SW3's junction blend made a linked branch's whole first segment transparent.
- Where a line runs on into a lake, pond or swamp:
  - Its channel shallows to nothing at its end (`MouthTaper`), so the bed meets the basin's shelf instead of ending in a pit.
  - Its ribbon is trimmed by distance along the line past the shoreline: rivers 16 m with a 13 m fade, streams 8 m with a 5 m fade. The river starts 13 m inside the home pond and fades in over 8 m.
  - The flat sheet skips cells up that channel beyond the shore, and fades out under the channel near the crossing through the mesh's green vertex channel (`_VertexColorTransparency` on; -1 = untouched, 1 = invisible). Without it, either surface's hard edge showed through the other.
  - SW3 scales a ribbon's tip fade by its own segment count (max(2, length / max segment length)); the fades are set in metres from that. Streams use a 1000 m max segment length, so each has 2 segments and the same fade.
- Materials, one palette per kind of water: `Water/Water_Still.mat` (from SW3 Smooth: the lake, highland ponds and the waterfall's source pool; deep blue-green, clear shallows, caustics 1), `Water_Pond.mat` (the home pond keeps Komorebi's turquoise with warm cream shallows), `Water_Swamp.mat` (murky olive tea, nearly opaque from 1 m, a dark mirror, no caustics) and `Water_River.mat` (from SplineRiver Default: rivers and streams, clear and lighter). The river shares Still's horizon, refraction, reflections and translucency and has no waves; where a ribbon fades out over a lake or pond its end fade blends the two palettes. River mode needs vertex-colour transparency and foam on and intersection source 2.
- Reflections: off for performance. `WaterReflectionsOff` (called by `BuildWaterMaterials`) turns off Environment Reflections in every water material (`_EnvironmentReflectionsOn` 0 with the `_ENVIRONMENTREFLECTIONS_OFF` keyword: no sky, probe or screen-space reflections). Specular highlights (`_SpecularReflectionsOn`) and received shadows are off on every water material too, set by hand in bb665145e (the generator doesn't set them). The Great Lake's realtime probe (`Water/Lake Reflection`, `BuildLakeReflection()`) is kept but deactivated. No planar reflections: they re-render the whole terrain and its trees every frame and draw the ANGRY MESH range without fog.
- Calm flow: the vendor river material runs time at 16×. Ours uses `_Speed` 6, slope speed 1.5, foam speed 0.7 and ribbon foam velocity strength 0.45. The waterfall copy flows at 0.42×.
- Intersection source 2 adds the ribbon's vertex-colour edge band to the foam. In sharp mode everything above the clip turns white, which drew a continuous curb along both banks. `_IntersectionClipping` 0.6 keeps only the noise peaks. Don't set `_IntersectionLength` below 1: that zeroes the depth term.
- Gotcha: refreshing a mesh asset with `EditorUtility.CopySerialized` left the old GPU buffers on screen; `SaveMesh` refills it through the Mesh API instead (vertex colours included).

## Underwater

SW3's Underwater Rendering extension (render feature setting already on in `PC_Renderer`). `BuildUnderwater()` builds `Environment/Water/Underwater`:

- One `UnderwaterArea` (trigger box from 15 m under the surface to 3 m above, the shore bounds plus 10 m) per flat level: `Home Pond + River` (19.5 m, pond palette), `Great Lake` (19.5 m), `WestPond` (50 m), `NorthPond` (44 m), `Swamp` (24.5 m, fog density 1.2). The extension needs one flat water height per area, which is why the home pond and river sit at the lake's level. Streams and the outlet river slope, so they have none.
- The area's `Reset()` runs on `AddComponent` and resizes the box to 100 × 50 × 100 m; the builder sets the box afterwards.
- Still, pond and swamp materials render both faces (`_Cull` 0) so their surface shows from below.
- The vendor shader turns underwater shading off in river mode, so the river ribbon is invisible from below. `River Underside` (`Maps/WorldMap/Water/RiverUnderside.asset`) is a flat sheet over the channel at 19.5 m with `Water_RiverUnderside.mat` (a pond copy drawing back faces only, `_Cull` 1): seen from below only.
- Known gap: the waterfall sheet and other transparents draw after the underwater fog and stay unfogged.

## Waterfall

- Unity's detail system rejects prefabs with a LODGroup on the mesh root. `Vegetation/Details/*_Detail.prefab` are plain mesh copies; every grass and flower detail now goes through one (below), the ferns, leaves and stones still use vendor prefabs or their own copies.
- **Stylized Grass.** Use Stylized Grass for every new grass or flower detail from now on: add the prefab to `SGFamilies` (or give it an `SG_*` material copy the same way) instead of using the vendor BK or Raygeas grass shaders. All grass and flower details draw with Stylized Grass material copies (`Vegetation/Materials/SG_*`), on the same BK, Komorebi and HIGHLANDS meshes, stamps and density maps, matched to the old shaders from screenshots at fixed views (mean colour within a few RGB steps, contrast 90–100%):
  - **Meadow lawn and tall grass** (`Vegetation.StylizedMeadow(bool)`): BK/Grass colours = `lerp(sun, cool, noise) × texture`, with a 2048² noise tile repeating every 25 m (patches 0.3–2 m, so neighbouring blades differ). Stylized Grass can only lerp toward its colour map, so the colour map (`Vegetation/Textures/BK_Meadow_Colormap`, the lawn blend of that tile, 25 m bounds at the world origin, Repeat wrap) carries 70% of the colour and a contrast-boosted texture copy (`BK_Grass_a_SG`, × 1/(1 − 0.7)) carries the blade streaks; the tall grass takes 62% from the same map with its own base colour. `Environment/Grass Colormap` (`GrassColorMapRenderer`, `overrideTexture` on) activates it; never press its Render button.
  - **Other grass and flowers** (`Vegetation.StylizedDetails(bool)`, families in `SGFamilies`): BK flowers, sorrel and reeds take their colour at the mean noise (the white-tinted ones match exactly; pink heads lose their 100 m tint drift); Komorebi `Grass_1`, `Grass_1_Large` and clovers get their UV colour gradient baked into texture copies (`Vegetation/Textures/*_SG`), and the grass's 6 m world-noise patches of yellow tips become a per-clump tip tint (hue variation 0.85); salvia and the HIGHLANDS flower are colour × texture. The Raygeas families keep their smoothness (their sheen toward the sun). Meshes without vertex colours (Komorebi, HIGHLANDS) use copies in `Vegetation/Meshes/*_SG` with a root-to-tip mask in red, which Stylized Grass reads for wind and bending.
  - Wind is on the materials (yaw 30 like the wind drivers, gentle ambient sway and gusts), not a `WindController`.
  - **Bending.** Grass bends around anything drawn into Stylized Grass's bend map (50 m around the camera, `GrassRenderFeature` in `PC_Renderer`). The player carries its own `GrassBender` (`Grass Bender` child of `Player.prefab`, a 1.6 m sphere 0.1 m above the feet, flatten 1, push 2, so the grass you stand on lies flat and swept outward; `AgentScripts/Grass/PlayerGrassBender.cs`). Enemies, and animals later, join `Environment/Grass Bending` (`GrassBendField`: one particle-system bender, a sphere particle per mover moved each frame, flatten 1, push 2; `AgentScripts/Grass/GrassBendingSetup.cs` creates it and wires it into the `EnvironmentInstaller`). An enemy joins while active with `EnemyDefinition.GrassBendRadius` (0.6 m); a new mover kind calls `GrassBendField.Add(transform, radius)` / `Remove` the same way, never a `GrassBender` per crowd entity. `GraphicsService` keeps the render feature visible to game cameras (PLUGINS.md → Stylized Grass). Benders only reach the bend map while the main camera's culling sees their renderer, which is also where their grass is visible. Stylized Grass applies flatten twice (bend-map mask × offset), so low values barely show; at flatten 3 the blades sink into the ground and read as bare earth. Per material (`Vegetation.FlowerBending`, re-applied without a rebuild by `Vegetation.ApplyFlowerBending()`): grass bends per vertex at full strength; tall flowers (`TallFlowers`) bend uniformly from their pivot at 0.5, because per-vertex bending tore their heads apart; the flat ground flowers (`GroundFlowers`: daisy, blue and pink heads, sorrel, bend mask 1 down to the root) don't bend, since a bender could only slide or sink the whole card.
  - `StylizedMeadow(false)` / `StylizedDetails(false)` put the old materials and meshes back (the stamps keep the `_Game` prefabs, which look the same), for A/B and rollback. `Vegetation.Build()` calls both with `true`.
  - Cost (2026-10-26, `PerfSuite.Views` at the nine test views, old/new alternated twice, editor play mode, RTX 5070 Ti Laptop): no frame-time change (9.22 → 9.21 ms mean, about 108 fps both); FrameTiming GPU +0.02–0.13 ms per view (swamp the most); SetPass +5–10; draws and triangles unchanged.
- BK plants need `BK Foliage Globals` (`_Core/EnvironmentSystem/BKFoliageGlobals`, on `Environment/Wind`, render distance 240 = 2× the detail distance). Without it they fade out beyond about 50 m and have no micro wind.
- **Clearing**: two ClearStamps last in the group, masked by `Veg_ClearTrees` / `Veg_ClearDetails`. `GenerateClearMasks` builds them from the scene: footprints of outcrops, waterfall rocks, boundary foot rocks, meadow stones, bank and shore rocks, the yard (trees 7–13 m off the fence, in the yard frame), the spawn→bridge sight line (no crowns), the house and props, the bridge, and trail cores (the frayed half-width). Re-run it after moving rocks or landmarks.
- Noise (MicroVerse FBM): n spans about ±0.47 (p10/p90 ±0.14); weight = saturate(1 + (n − balance) × amplitude). Tree stamps keep each candidate with probability = weight.
- Rendering: Unity's terrain renderer (saved scene: tree distance 1000, detail distance 100, terrain shadows off; a leftover `GPUITerrainBuiltin` sits on the terrain with no GPUI manager). Keep the detail distance at or below 120: details are most of the triangles (250 m drew 20M triangles at the spawn, 120 m about 9.5M), and the ferns (360–760 triangles), Farfugium (509) and clovers (228) are the heaviest prototypes. The 2026-10-20 trim (Salvia swap, merged layers) cut detail triangles within 120 m by 27% at the spawn, 14% in the meadow, 9% in the highlands and 4% in the forest. GPU Instancer Pro is not set up yet; see "Open decisions".

## Atmosphere

The light, ambient, fog, sky and grade below are the **09:00 look** of the day/night cycle ([DAYNIGHT.md](DAYNIGHT.md)). In Play mode `Environment/Time Of Day` (`EnvironmentDirector`) drives them from the world clock: the sun moves, the moon takes over at night, and the god rays, birds, butterflies, motes, mist and fireflies follow the hours. The values here seed `TimeOfDay/Looks/TOD_Morning.asset` and the `PP_Day` grade.

- Post-processing: `Data/Environment/TimeOfDay/Base_PostProcessing.asset` (copy of Komorebi's demo grade, toned down: contrast 20, saturation +2, white balance +10, bloom 0.3, vignette 0.25; lens flare and motion blur off) is the source of the day grade. `Atmosphere.ClonePostProcessing()` still puts it on a `Post-Processing` volume, but the day/night setup merges it into the time-of-day grades and removes that volume ([DAYNIGHT.md → Look of the day](DAYNIGHT.md#look-of-the-day), the Grades bullet).
- The player prefab renders no post-processing. This scene overrides `PlayerCamera` to `renderPostProcessing = true`. Held models draw before post-processing (`FirstPersonRenderFeature`), so world and weapon get one grade. The Player prefab asset is unchanged.
- Sun 32° / 300° (`SunPitch`/`SunYaw`), 5000 K, 2.8, soft shadows, Komorebi cloud-shadow cookie (500 m) drifting with `Raygeas.CloudMover`. The low sun from the east-south-east side-lights the home→bridge view and back-lights the meadow's east view. A shadowless sky-bounce fill light from the north lifts the north-facing cliffs.
- Project settings (`Assets/_Game/Settings/PC_RPAsset`, `PC_Renderer`, `QualitySettings`), applying to every scene:
  - Main-light shadows to 150 m, 4 cascades, 2048 map (same cost as 100 m in A/B; the URP template's 50 m left every tree past it without a shadow).
  - SSAO off and decals in Screen Space (bullet holes). SSAO's depth-normals source and DBuffer decals (what Automatic picks on desktop) each force a depth-normals prepass that re-draws every grass blade: about 4k draws, 8M triangles and 2–4 ms GPU at the spawn.
  - Depth priming Forced: a depth-only prepass, then opaques shade only visible pixels. It halves GPU time where foliage overlaps (forest 7.1 → 3.9 ms, meadow 5.5 → 3.7 ms) for about 1k extra draws.
  - LOD bias 0.7. Bias 2 doubled every LOD and cull distance (about 4k extra draws at the spawn); 0.7–0.75 switches LODs and culls a quarter sooner, picked from A/B runs at the test views.
  - GPU Resident Drawer small-mesh culling at 0.3% of screen height (`PC_RPAsset` → Small-Mesh Screen-Percentage) skips pebbles and far props that cover a few pixels. The shadow-pass version gave nothing and stays at 0.
  - Static batching off (Player Settings, Standalone). Static-batched renderers point at a combined mesh, which the GPU Resident Drawer can't instance. It also duplicated about 4.6M vertices (≈263 MB). Turning it off removed about 320 draws at the same CPU and GPU cost. Objects keep their other static flags.
  - Mip streaming off (`QualitySettings` → PC). No texture has Stream Mip Maps on, so streaming only cost `TextureStreamingManager.UpdateRenderers` walking the 13k renderers: about 0.5 ms main thread per frame. About 2.5 GB of textures are resident in Play mode; if VRAM gets tight, turn streaming back on together with Stream Mip Maps on the big textures.
- Sky `TimeOfDay/TimeOfDay_Sky.mat` (`Aipocalypse/Skybox/Time Of Day`): the Komorebi painted panorama of `Base_Skybox.mat` (rotation 17, so its painted sun glow sits behind the 09:00 light) with the time of day on top. `Base_Skybox.mat` is kept but no longer assigned. Fog ExponentialSquared 0.0011, (0.58, 0.76, 0.94). Trilight ambient.
- `Environment/Atmosphere`: 24 god rays aligned to the sun (their beam axis follows `SunPitch`), mist over clearings, the lake, river and stream mouths, swamp fog, 10 cloud particles around the rim, high and local bird flocks, butterflies over the meadow, leaf fall under the broadleafs, dandelion seed fluff over the meadow and the yard (Kroma, camera-follow and collision off), gold motes in the god-ray clearings and over the pond island (SICS `TEM_Dust_01A`), and fireflies in shaded stands near the forest trails and at the swamp's wooded edge (SICS `TFF_Fireflies_01A`). Edits to these vendor systems are prefab overrides recorded in `Tune`; their culling is Pause. No dragonfly asset is installed.
- `Environment/Wind`: the three Raygeas wind drivers at yaw 30 (sun yaw + 90), and `BK Foliage Globals` for the BK plants.

## Open decisions and known issues

- **Grass bending** (Stylized Grass → Bending) is tuned from the player's eye looking down (same-frame A/B renders in play mode). Not yet checked in a build or with many enemies.

- **GPUI.** Project rules say GPU Instancer Pro draws foliage. Doing that needs `_Game` copies of the Raygeas Shader Graphs with GPUI's setup node (otherwise magenta), and a decision on GPUI's generated `*_GPUIPro.shader` files next to vendor shaders. Unity's terrain renderer draws each tree separately: about 6k of 9.6k draw calls at the spawn before the settings above, and 4.5–7.8 ms GPU in the forest and highlands.
- **Performance (2026-10-20, editor play mode, 1080p, RTX 5070 Ti Laptop).** Target 80–100 fps on this machine. Nine test views (spawn both ways, meadow → lake, lakeside viewpoint, two forest views, two highland views, swamp) run at 107–131 fps, up from 83–113 before the pass below. Main thread is 5.0–6.5 ms, of which the editor loop is about 3 ms; GPU is 1.5–2.8 ms. Draw calls at the spawn fell from 4.1k to 2.5k. The pass:
  - first-person rendering moved from an overlay camera into a renderer feature (about 1.1 ms CPU);
  - static batching off and GRD small-mesh culling;
  - LOD bias 0.75;
  - the detail trim;
  - shadows off on bank and water rocks;
  - Pause culling on the god-ray, bird, butterfly and leaf-fall particles (their noise module made Automatic culling simulate them off-screen).
  Measuring scripts (play mode, `script run`): `AgentScripts/Perf/` (`PerfSuite` views / actions / first use / day-night / A/B / GC stacks, `PerfSettings` the project settings of the 2026-10-25 pass, `SceneScan` static dump; the older `PerfProbe`, `PerfAB`, `PerfCapture`); method and traps in tools-unity-map-generation `references/performance.md`.
- **Performance (2026-10-25, editor play mode, 1080p, Core Ultra 7 255HX + RTX 5070 Ti Laptop, Unity focused).** Test views (`PerfSuite.DefaultViews`, `name:x,z,yaw`, `spawn` = the player's spawn): `spawn_bridge:spawn,50; spawn_house:spawn,230; meadow_east:420,250,90; lake_view:526,196,100; forest_in:450,540,20; forest_lake:590,400,120; highland:330,690,80; highland_top:290,760,160; swamp_edge:600,790,60`.

The editor loop adds about 2.3 ms. GPU is 1.3–2.7 ms. The main thread is the limit everywhere; the GPU has room even at a 2060 (roughly 2.5× slower).
  - Gameplay scripts are cheap (`Player.Update` ~0.2 ms). The rest of the update is engine and package work: ECS systems for A* and Behavior Designer (~1 ms in the editor for two enemies), GPU Resident Drawer ~0.4 ms, physics ~0.3 ms, terrain details ~0.45 ms.
  - Night (moon too dim to cast shadows) is the cheapest time. 17:00–18:00 is the heaviest: the low sun gives ~1.4k shadow casters and ~2.9k draws (+0.4 ms). A look transition adds 0.3–0.5 ms during its 5 s glide. The worn lantern costs nothing measurable.

A/B at the spawn (GPU on this machine): 2 shadow cascades −0.28 ms, soft shadows Medium −0.07 ms, render scale 0.8 −0.15 ms. GPU occlusion culling is worse: +1.1 ms GPU and +0.9 ms CPU.   - First-use hitches: a clip with Preload Audio Data off loaded on its first play, about 11 ms each (pistol reload 21.5 → 9.7 ms worst frame after the fix). URP's decal chunk resized (2.5–5 ms) each time bullet holes regrew from none, because an empty chunk is dropped; holes no longer time out (WEAPONS.md), so it resizes only while the first 64 fill. Still open: the first gun pickup (~10 ms spawning the held item and prewarming its effects) and about 5 ms starting the effects on the first shots.

Recording the profiler adds `Profiler.FlushMemoryCounters` (~2.4 ms) and Addressables' profiler emitter spikes (45–60 ms). An Inspector showing a material re-validates vendor effect materials on the first shot (revert that churn, see below).
